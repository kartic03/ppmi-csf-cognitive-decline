# Figure captions

Every numeral below is read from the source artifact at runtime by `make_captions.py`; none is transcribed by hand. Artifact integrity verdicts referenced here are recorded in `ARTIFACT_INTEGRITY_SWEEP.md`.

## Figure 1. The CSF increment against its null.

**(a)** Adding the CSF block to the pre-registered 10-variable clinical baseline raises out-of-fold R² for the continuous decline-rate outcome from 0.0692 to 0.1273 (ΔR² = +0.0581); against a stronger 14-variable clinical block the same CSF panel raises R² from 0.2106 to 0.2562 (ΔR² = +0.0456). A richer clinical baseline absorbs part of the increment without eliminating it. Both rows come from `phase2/estimand_audit.json` (`results['eb_slope|thin']` and `results['eb_slope|enriched']`), whose control arm reproduces the committed headline in `phase2/increment.json` to the last digit. `phase2/enriched_baseline_check.json` is deliberately NOT used: its control arm returns ΔR² = 0.05079576, which is exactly `increment.json rq1_csf.perm_observed_dr2_fixed` (0.05079576) — the fixed-alpha permutation estimator, not the tuned nested-CV estimator its own configuration declares. **(b)** The observed CSF ΔR² of 0.0581 (`phase2/increment.json`, `rq1_csf.dr2`) against 20 random-feature draws (`phase2/noise_floor_distribution.json`), which have mean -0.0040 and SD 0.0067; the observed value sits 9.3 SD above the null mean and far beyond the null's 95th percentile of 0.0078. The error bar is the subject-level percentile bootstrap over participants, 0.0120 to 0.0923 (B = 200, `phase2/bootstrap_ci.json`) — the only confidence interval on the increment anywhere in this figure set. Cross-seed repeat ranges are not confidence intervals and are not drawn. n = 607 CSF-complete participants.

## Figure 2. Where the increment holds and where it fails.

**(a)** ΔR² from adding the CSF block, by stratum, ordered so the two negative cells sit immediately beneath the overall result rather than at the foot of the panel. Overall ΔR² = +0.0581 (n = 607, `phase2/increment.json`). The increment REVERSES in the youngest age tertile (ΔR² = -0.0509, n = 203) and is absent in the administratively censored cell (ΔR² = -0.0153, n = 125). It holds in completers (+0.0067, n = 263), the middle tertile (+0.0451, n = 202), the oldest tertile (+0.1097, n = 202) and early exiters (+0.1193, n = 219). Age-stratified increments come from `phase2/batch1_checks.json` (`age_tertile_dr2`), whose `noise_block` section is superseded by `phase2/noise_floor_distribution.json` but whose age arm is untouched by that supersession and is independently corroborated: all three per-tertile augmented R² reproduce `phase3/fairness_audit.json`'s age-tertile R² to ~1e-16 at identical n. Visit strata come from `phase2/phase1_visit_split.json` and are the PHASE-STRATIFIED cells; the file's pooled '(all phases)' cells are not plotted, because the pooled few-visit cell (n = 344) is the sum of the two phase cells shown and would double-count the phase-2 participants. Each stratification partitions the cohort exactly. These cells are scored on shared out-of-fold predictions and carry no confidence intervals. **(b)** The SAME CSF block on the DICHOTOMISED conversion endpoint, a different metric on a separate axis: AUC moves from 0.630 to 0.627 (-0.003) against the clinical block, and from 0.676 to 0.673 (-0.003) against the enriched block. CSF adds nothing on this endpoint. Source `phase2/hlavnicka_head2head.json` (n = 324, 127 events), which is **UNANCHORABLE**: it shares no arm with any committed value, so unlike every other source here it cannot be checked against the headline result. Its brackets are cross-seed ranges, not confidence intervals, and are not drawn.

## Figure 3. Calibration.

**(a)** Binned decile calibration curve: 10 bins of 60–61 participants each (summing to 607), plotting mean observed against mean predicted decline rate, with the 45° ideal. Each point is a BIN MEAN, not a participant. Coordinates come from `phase2/calibration.json` (`continuous.calibration_curve`), the only artifact in the project holding calibration curve coordinates of any kind. The overall calibration slope is 1.0058 (`phase2/calibration_flexible.json`, `overall_slope`; intercept -0.000350), and the flexible calibration mean absolute deviation is 0.026472 (same file, `flexible_mean_abs_deviation`). That flexible result exists ONLY as a summary statistic — no flexible-fit coordinates exist anywhere in `data/processed` — so no smooth fit is drawn and the panel must not be read as showing one. **(b)** Per-age-tertile calibration slopes against the ideal of 1: the youngest tertile collapses to 0.271 (n = 203), while the middle and oldest tertiles are close to ideal at 1.086 and 1.119. **(c)** The same three tertiles' ΔR² from Figure 2, on the same rows: the n = 203 youngest subgroup fails on both axes — its calibration slope collapses AND its CSF increment reverses (-0.0509). These are two independent symptoms in one subgroup, not two unrelated findings. All three tertile slopes reconcile with `phase3/fairness_audit.json` to ~1e-16 at identical n, and the two calibration sources agree at the stored 4 dp of the coarser one (`calibration.json` `calib_slope` = 1.0058).

## Figure 4. Internal-external cross-validation across enrolment sites.

This is internal-external cross-validation across enrolment sites, NOT external validation: every fold shares PPMI's protocol, era and inclusion criteria. **(a)** Holding out whole enrolment sites lowers overall performance — clinical+CSF R² falls from 0.1273 under standard k-fold to 0.1150 under a matched random-cluster control and 0.0791 under leave-one-site-out, with the clinical baseline falling correspondingly from 0.0692 to 0.0625 to 0.0351. **(b)** The INCREMENT largely holds across the same three designs: +0.0581 (k-fold), +0.0525 (random-cluster, 5 seeds), +0.0440 (leave-one-site-out). The drop in (a) is a transportability cost paid by the whole model, not by the CSF block specifically. **(c)** Random-effects calibration slope across REAL SITES ONLY (k = 10): 0.777, 95% CI 0.542–1.012, 95% prediction interval for a new site 0.500–1.054. I² = 0% is UNDERPOWERED at k = 10 and must not be read as evidence of homogeneity. The k = 11 figure (0.765) is not used anywhere: it folds in a pooled cluster of 38 small sites (n = 289) that is not a site and, having the smallest standard error, would dominate the pooling. That cluster is retained in the pooled R² so no participant is dropped, but excluded from the calibration meta-analysis. Per-site R² is not plotted: the 10 real sites have n = 24–75, where it is noise. All values from `phase2/iecv_site.json`; n = 607 across 11 clusters.

## Figure 5. Controls against the random-feature null.

ΔR² for each control arm against the null band spanned by the 20 random-feature draws (-0.0115 to +0.0133), with the null's 95th percentile at 0.0078 marked. The CSF block under test contributes +0.0581 (n = 607). All three negative controls stay inside the null: 5 random Gaussians +0.0066, SAA assay version (technical/batch) +0.0008, and SAA positivity -0.0012. Of the two established genetic factors, APOE ε4 does not fire at all (-0.0003, n = 808) — a reportable result, not a missing bar: a well-established risk factor adds nothing to this decline-rate model. GBA carrier status contributes +0.0116 (n = 816), which clears the null 95th percentile but sits BELOW the largest single noise draw (0.0133) — clearing a percentile is not the same as exceeding every draw. Its contribution is also prevalence-bounded: at the observed carrier prevalence of 10.2% the maximum attainable R² contribution is 0.0227 (`phase2/gba_scale_anchor.json`), drawn on the panel, so the bar reads as capped by design rather than as a weak effect. Control arms from `phase2/rank11_controls.json`, whose anchor arm reproduces the committed increment exactly; null from `phase2/noise_floor_distribution.json`. Arms differ in n because each control is scored on the largest set where it is defined. The file's repeat brackets are cross-seed ranges, not confidence intervals, and are not drawn.

## Figure 6. The causal null.

Two-sample Mendelian randomisation for PDGFRB on both proteomic platforms, with the GPNMB positive control on the same odds-ratio axis. PDGFRB is null on deCODE (OR 0.992, 95% CI 0.961–1.024, 5 instruments, F = 13779) and on UKB-PPP (OR 0.973, 95% CI 0.922–1.027, 4 instruments, F = 2252). Two distinct quantities bound this null and are reported separately. The **equivalence bound** — the upper 95% confidence limit, i.e. the largest effect the data exclude — is OR 1.024254 on deCODE and OR 1.026742 on UKB-PPP; effects larger than these are ruled out. The **minimum detectable effect at 80% power** — the smallest odds ratio each design could have detected, computed in the source artifact as exp((z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₈₀) × SE) and marked below each interval — is OR 1.046433 and OR 1.080332 respectively. The first says what the data rule out; the second says what the study was capable of seeing, and for a null result the second is the more informative. Both sit far below the positive-control effect of OR 1.492 (1.289–1.727) that the same pipeline recovers on deCODE, so this is a null at a scale where the pipeline demonstrably detects a real effect, not a null from insufficient power. **The UKB-PPP arm is marked UNGATED on the figure.** The pre-specified design required positive-control recovery on BOTH platforms before any null could be reported; GPNMB does not recover on UKB-PPP (OR 0.994, 95% CI 0.896–1.102, spanning 1), so the gate was narrowed to deCODE only and nothing demonstrates the UKB-PPP arm could have detected a true effect. That arm is descriptive, and both it and the failed positive control are drawn with open markers and labelled UNGATED. Estimates and bounds from `mr/mr_power_bounds.json`; positive control from `mr/poscontrol_result.json`. `mr/mr_final_table.csv` is **UNANCHORABLE** — it contains no GPNMB row and so cannot be checked against either positive control — and is used here only as a 3 dp cross-check of the odds ratios, never as the plotted source.

### Sources flagged UNANCHORABLE where they are used

Two artifacts in this figure set share no arm with any committed value and therefore cannot be verified against the headline result. Both are flagged in the captions above at the point of use:

- `phase2/hlavnicka_head2head.json` (Figure 2b) — different endpoint, different sample (n = 324), different metric.
- `mr/mr_final_table.csv` (Figure 6, cross-check only) — no GPNMB row, so neither positive control appears in it.

A third, `phase2/trial_enrichment_ci.json`, is also UNANCHORABLE and is not used in this figure set. Note that CSF does not drive the trial-enrichment benefit: its incremental contribution straddles zero at every threshold.
